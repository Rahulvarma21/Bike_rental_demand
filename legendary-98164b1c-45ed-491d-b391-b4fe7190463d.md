## Revealing Different Demand Patterns by Day Type

### Separating working-day and off-day demand

In this step, I'm separating the records by day type. The comparistion reveals where one all-week schedule loses useful detail. The calculat seperate hourly averages for both day types, plot those patterns on one labeled chart and then interpret why each day type needs its own operating schedule.

![Image](https://nextwork.ai/chamarthi-raahul/uploads/98164b1c-45ed-491d-b391-b4fe7190463d_r2vldak3)

### Why an all-week schedule falls short

I obeserved, the highest point of the Working_day line is at the time around time 17(5pm), this due to the working-day line peaks at commuting periods, as it is a evening time most of the people at leaving the offices, and working places going home.  The offday (the weekend or holiday) line builds toward a later daytime peak. Why would one all-week schedule miss useful demand detail?

Missed-peak periods: It may underestimate demand during working-day commuting hours and fail to capture the earlier off-day demand pattern.
Poor bike availability: There might not be enough bikes at commuting locations during weekday rush hours, while leisure areas may need more bikes around midday on weekends.
Inefficient redistribution: Moving bikes based on an overall hourly average may not prepare the system for the different needs of each day type.

Conclusion: Separating working days from weekends and holidays helps bike-sharing operators forecast demand more accurately, position bikes where they are needed, and plan maintenance and redistribution more efficiently.
